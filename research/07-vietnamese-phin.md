## 1. Mechanism

The phin is a small **perforated metal drip chamber** that sits directly on top of the serving cup or glass. Components: a saucer/base plate, a brewing **chamber** with a perforated bottom, a loose **insert (gravity press) or screw-down press** that rests on the coffee bed, and a **lid**. There is **no paper filter** — the metal perforations are the only filter. [PDG; Wiki-CPSD]

How it brews:

1. Coffee grounds go in the chamber; the press disc is set on top of the bed.
2. A small amount of hot water is added first to **wet/bloom** the grounds (~45–60 s) so they swell and degas. [PDG]
3. The chamber is filled with the remaining hot water and capped.
4. Water percolates **slowly under gravity** through the bed and out the perforated base, dripping into the cup over several minutes. [PDG; Wiki-CPSD]

**Why it behaves differently from a V60 pour-over:**

- **No paper** → oils, colloids, and fines pass into the cup, giving a heavier, syrupy body and a layer of crema/foam (especially with robusta). A V60's paper strips oils/fines for a clean, light cup. [Wiki-Robusta; cf. methods doc V60]
- **Metal filter + a press disc holding the bed flat** → flow is **very slow and restricted**. Water pools above the bed for much of the brew, so the phin behaves like a **hybrid between percolation and immersion** (near-immersion), unlike the V60's continuous fresh-water percolation. [convention; PDG]
- **Self-paced, hands-off** → once filled and capped, the brewer doesn't pour in stages; contact time is set by grind + press tightness, not by pour technique. This makes it slower (often 4–6 min) than a V60's 2:30–3:30. [PDG; convention]

The press disc is the key control surface: pressed tighter (or screwed down), it slows the drip and lengthens contact; loose, it drips faster.

## 2. Typical Parameters

- **Grind size:**
  - *Traditional:* **medium-coarse**, comparable to coarse sand / sea salt — coarse enough that the slow metal filter still drains, and matched to robusta's dark roast. [convention]
  - *Modern specialty:* trending **medium** (toward "medium-coarse to medium") for arabica or arabica-robusta blends to lift extraction without choking the filter. [PDG; convention]
- **Dose:** ~**2–3 Tbsp ≈ 12–25 g** per phin; a common single-serve dose is **~15–20 g** filling the chamber roughly one-third to half. [convention]
- **Ratio:** brewed **strong**, roughly **1:6 to 1:10** coffee:water (much more concentrated than the SCA 1:15–1:18 filter band) because it is designed to be cut with condensed milk and/or ice. A black (đen) phin runs nearer the upper, weaker end. [convention; Wiki-CPSD]
- **Water temperature:** near-boiling, **~90–96 °C** (Vietnamese practice often uses water straight off the boil, ~95 °C). [convention; cf. Wiki-Extraction temp guidance]
- **Bloom step:** **yes** — pre-infuse with a small splash, wait **~45–60 s** for the bed to swell and degas before filling. [PDG]
- **Total brew/drip time:** **~4–6 minutes**, deliberately slow; tightening the press lengthens it. Notably slower than pour-over. [PDG; convention]

## 3. Bean: Robusta (traditional) vs. Arabica (modern specialty)

**Traditional = Robusta (*Coffea canephora*).** Vietnam is the world's largest robusta producer and grows ~97% robusta; the phin was built around it. [PDG; Wiki-CPSD]

Robusta vs. arabica, as it matters for the cup:

- **Caffeine:** robusta ~**2.7%** vs. arabica ~**1.5%** by mass — roughly **double**. [Wiki-Robusta] A brewed cup of coffee broadly ranges ~50–175 mg caffeine depending on bean and method, and robusta sits at the high end. [Wiki-Caffeine]
- **Flavor:** robusta has **lower acidity, more bitterness**, and a **woody / earthy, less fruity** profile, with more bitterness attributed to its **pyrazine** content. [Wiki-Robusta]
- **Crema/foam:** robusta is prized for producing a **fuller body and a better foam head (crema)** — the phin yields a visible crema layer. [Wiki-Robusta]
- **Sugar:** robusta has **less sugar** (~3–7%) than arabica (~6–9%), reinforcing perceived bitterness/lack of sweetness. [Wiki-Robusta]
- **Roast:** traditionally **dark-roasted longer** (masks ripeness variation); flavorings such as **butter, sugar, vanilla, and cocoa may be added during the roast**, and some street vendors add starches/extra butter to thicken the brew. This "butter/oil" roast style is characteristic of Vietnamese robusta. [PDG]

**Modern specialty phin** uses **arabica or arabica–robusta blends** and lighter, cleaner roasts. As Annee Nguyen notes, blends of robusta and arabica "can still give you the bold, bitter, and chocolatey tastes" that pair with condensed milk while adding more complexity. Specialty roasters push grind slightly finer (toward medium) and ratios a touch weaker to showcase origin character rather than just strength. [PDG]

## 4. Serving Traditions

- **cà phê sữa đá** (iced coffee with condensed milk): the phin drips directly onto a layer of **sweetened condensed milk**, then it is stirred and poured over ice. The **bitterness of dark-roast robusta balances the intense sweetness** of the condensed milk — the two were made for each other. [Wiki-CPSD; PDG]
- **Why condensed milk:** a **colonial-era practicality** — fresh dairy was scarce/spoiled fast in the tropical climate, so shelf-stable imported sweetened condensed milk became the substitute, and it stuck. Ice was added for the hot climate. [Wiki-CPSD]
- **cà phê đen / đen đá** (black, hot or iced): strong plain phin coffee, no milk, sometimes lightly sugared — leans into robusta's intensity and bitterness. [Wiki-CPSD]
- The resulting drink is reported as **"sweet, thick, and strong"** with a dense mouthfeel, chocolate notes, and lingering bitterness. [PDG]

## 5. Resulting Cup Profile & SCA Brewing Control Chart Placement

- **Body:** **heavy / syrupy** — no paper, plus oils and fines from dark robusta, plus a crema layer. [Wiki-Robusta; PDG]
- **Strength:** **high** — the low brew ratio (~1:6–1:10) puts undiluted phin TDS **well above** the SCA filter target of ~**1.15–1.45%** strength. [Wiki-Extraction] (It is then diluted by milk/ice in cà phê sữa đá.)
- **Bitterness:** **high**, from dark roast + robusta pyrazines + the long, slow, near-immersion contact time. [Wiki-Robusta; PDG]
- **Extraction yield:** the long contact and hot water push extraction toward — and often **past** — the SCA's ideal **18–22%** band; phin commonly trends **high/over-extracted** by filter-coffee standards, but that bitterness is **intentional** and balanced by sweetness. [Wiki-Extraction; PDG]

**On the SCA brewing control chart** (axes: strength/TDS vs. extraction yield), phin sits in the **upper-right region — high strength and high-to-over extraction** — outside the central "ideal" rectangle that pour-over/drip target. That position is a feature of the style, not a fault: the cup is engineered to be strong and bitter so condensed milk can balance it. [Wiki-Extraction; interpretation]

## 6. Defaults & Adjustable Ranges for the Simulator

Suggested **default** (a classic cà phê sữa đá-style strong black brew before milk):

| Parameter | Default | Adjustable range | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bean | Dark-roast robusta | robusta ↔ arabica-robusta blend ↔ specialty arabica | Drives bitterness, caffeine, crema, body |
| Grind | Medium-coarse | medium ↔ coarse | Primary contact-time lever; finer = slower drip, more extraction |
| Dose | 18 g | 12–25 g | Fills chamber ~⅓–½ |
| Ratio | 1:8 | 1:6 (intense) ↔ 1:10 (đen, lighter) | Sets strength/TDS |
| Water temp | 95 °C | 90–96 °C | Off-boil typical |
| Bloom | 50 s | 30–60 s | Wet the bed, then fill |
| Press tightness | Medium | loose ↔ tight (screw) | Sets drip speed / contact time |
| Total drip time | ~5 min | 4–6+ min | Consequence of grind + press |
| Roast | Dark | medium ↔ very dark | Dark adds bitterness/body; "butter roast" optional flavor flag |
| Serve | + condensed milk + ice | black (đen) ↔ sữa đá | Dilution + sweetness balance |

**Variables that matter most (rank for the simulator):**

1. **Press tightness + grind** — together they set drip speed and thus contact time / extraction (the phin's main extraction lever, analogous to grind in a V60).
2. **Bean + roast** — robusta vs. arabica and roast depth dominate bitterness, caffeine, and crema/body.
3. **Ratio** — sets strength/TDS independent of extraction (and how much milk it can carry).
4. **Water temperature** and **bloom** — secondary fine-tuning of extraction.

## 7. How It Compares to Other Methods

In one or two sentences: the **phin is a slow, paperless, near-immersion metal drip** that yields a heavy-bodied, strong, bitter cup — closer in spirit to a **French press** (immersion, oils/fines pass, full body) than to a **V60** (fast paper percolation, clean and bright), while its undiluted strength and crema-topped intensity sit between filter coffee and **espresso** (which is far more concentrated and pressure-driven). Where V60 is dialed for clarity and acidity inside the SCA ideal box, the phin is deliberately pushed into the strong/high-extraction corner and then balanced by condensed milk and ice.
